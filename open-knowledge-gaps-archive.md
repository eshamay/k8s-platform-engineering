## Full lifecycle trace from Git commit to Ready status — status: closed (2026-11-26)

- **Question:** What exactly happens, step by step, from a Git commit in a Flux-managed repository through source fetch, rendering, server-side apply, finalizer registration, controller reconciliation, and status condition updates until the object reports Ready?
- **Why it matters:** The lifecycle trace ties together nearly every concept in the vocabulary and is the best test of whether the wiki's concept pages compose into a coherent whole.
- **Owner:** unassigned
- **How to close:**
  - [ ] Find sources that describe Flux's source-controller, kustomize-controller, and helm-controller flow in detail.
  - [ ] Trace SSA field ownership, finalizer, and condition behavior through the flow.
  - [ ] Write a worked example page under `examples/`.
- **Drafted by:** agent on 2026-11-25
- **Tags:** flux, lifecycle, ssa, finalizers, conditions
- **Resolution:** Settled at the level of an assembled trace, not a single-source trace. Part 5 sections 2 to 4 ([[outputs/part-5-platform-architecture-principles-and-operating-at-scale]]) now walk one merged commit end to end: the source controller fetches the repository on its interval (or immediately when a notification Receiver fires) and packages an artifact; the Kustomization builds and applies it with server-side apply under a Flux field manager, runs an apply dry run each interval to detect drift, and impersonates a tenant ServiceAccount; built-in controllers then carry the intent forward through owner references. For the Crossplane half, the trace continues through the composite, the composition-function pipeline, managed resources and the provider's Connect, Observe, Create, Update, Delete cycle, and returns upward through status conditions (Ready, Reconciling, Stalled, Synced) read together with observedGeneration. Supporting pages: [[entities/flux]], [[concepts/server-side-apply-and-field-ownership]], [[concepts/finalizers]], [[concepts/conditions]], [[examples/gitops-reverts-a-hand-edited-deployment]]. Not settled: no single source traces this flow, so the trace is the wiki's own composition of documented behaviors and says where documentation is silent; finalizer behavior is covered on the deletion path (Crossplane managed resources, Flux prune and deletion policy), not as a registration step on create; and the how-to-close item for a worked example under examples/ was met by Part 5 instead of a standalone page. Remaining questions are tracked in [[open-knowledge-gaps#flux-source-controller-internals-and-artifact-serving-under-leader-election]] and [[#flux-kustomization-finalizer-name-and-behavior]].

## Crossplane Compositions vs kro dynamic resource graphs — status: closed (2026-11-26)

- **Question:** How do Crossplane Compositions (including composition functions) and kro's ResourceGraphDefinition differ in how they model, order, and reconcile dynamic resource graphs, and what trade-offs follow?
- **Why it matters:** Both offer a simplified API over many underlying resources, but with different execution models; the choice affects portability, debuggability, and dependency handling.
- **Owner:** unassigned
- **How to close:**
  - [ ] Collect official docs and design documents for both tools.
  - [ ] Collect practitioner comparisons and real-world adoption stories.
  - [ ] Write a `comparisons/` page.
- **Drafted by:** agent on 2026-11-25
- **Tags:** crossplane, kro, composition, abstraction
- **Resolution:** Settled as a structured comparison with a named decision. [[comparisons/crossplane-composition-functions-versus-kro-resource-graph-execution-models]] sets the two side by side: Crossplane splits schema (XRD) from implementation (Composition) and runs a pipeline of gRPC functions in any language, leaving ordering to the functions and eventual reconciliation, with readiness from steps such as function-auto-ready and errors surfacing mostly at reconcile time; kro puts schema and implementation in one ResourceGraphDefinition, evaluates CEL expressions, infers a dependency graph from field references, creates in topological order and deletes in reverse, gates dependents with readyWhen, and validates at creation time. [[comparisons/where-the-expressiveness-bound-sits-in-crossplane-functions-and-kro-cel]] explains the underlying trade (Crossplane bounds the contract and frees the code; kro bounds the language and frees the runtime), [[comparisons/crossplane-xrds-versus-kro-resourcegraphdefinitions-as-crd-generators]] covers how each generates its API, and [[decisions/choose-kro-crossplane-or-both-for-a-platform-abstraction-layer]] records the choice, including the function-kro bridge ([[entities/function-kro]]) that lets the two combine. Part 4 ([[outputs/part-4-platform-abstractions]]) teaches it. Not settled: the comparison is the wiki's synthesis, because no source compares the two head to head; kro is pre-1.0 (v1alpha1, version 0.10.0 in the docs read) so its facts may change; and kro dependency and readiness mechanics, status schema, released-version facts and per-RGD scale remain open in [[#kro-dependency-graph-and-readiness-mechanics]], [[open-knowledge-gaps#kro-status-schema-and-crossplane-v2-status-propagation]], [[#kro-graph-api-status-and-released-version-facts]] and [[open-knowledge-gaps#scaling-limits-of-per-rgd-kro-controllers-and-named-production-kro-adopters]].
